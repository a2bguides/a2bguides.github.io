> **Last checked: 12 October 2026.** Prices and rules change; check the linked sources before relying on them.

If you have been sending invoices from a Word document or a spreadsheet, an invoicing app can let you create and send them from your phone and give customers a way to pay by card. This guide compares the free plans of three apps that publish UK pricing: Square Invoices, SumUp Invoices and Zoho Invoice. It sets out what each provider says its free plan includes, what the paid upgrade costs, and what each provider says it charges when a customer pays.

Every price, fee and limit below links to the provider's own page. Where a provider's page does not state something, we say so rather than guess.

## Who this is for

This guide is for UK sole traders and small trade or service businesses who:

- currently send invoices from a Word or spreadsheet template and want to create and send invoices from a phone
- want customers to be able to pay an invoice by card
- want to know what that will cost before signing up

It does not cover limited companies' invoicing rules, VAT invoices or tax. For those, go to GOV.UK (see the next section). If you are still happy with templates, our guide to [free quote and invoice templates for UK trades](/guides/quote-invoice-templates/) may be all you need.

## What a UK invoice must include, whichever app you use

An app helps you lay out an invoice, but you are still responsible for what goes on it. GOV.UK lists what an invoice must include ([GOV.UK: Invoices – what they must include](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include), checked 12 October 2026). In summary:

- a unique identification number
- your name, address and contact information (GOV.UK's wording is "your company name, address and contact information")
- the name and address of the customer you are invoicing (GOV.UK's wording is "the company name and address of the customer you're invoicing")
- a clear description of what you are charging for
- the date the goods or service were provided (the supply date)
- the date of the invoice
- the amount or amounts being charged
- the VAT amount, if applicable
- the total amount owed

GOV.UK adds that a sole trader's invoice must also include "your name and any business name being used", and "an address where any legal documents can be delivered to you if you are using a business name" (same page, checked 12 October 2026).

GOV.UK has separate sections on that page for limited companies and for VAT invoices. We do not give tax, VAT or Making Tax Digital advice. If any of those apply to you, read the GOV.UK page directly or speak to a qualified adviser.

## Square Invoices: free plan, Plus plan and card fees

All details in this section are from [Square Invoices](https://squareup.com/gb/en/invoices), checked 12 October 2026.

**Free plan.** Square lists the Free plan at "£0" with "No monthly fees". It says you can "Send unlimited invoices, estimates, contracts and more for free."

**Plus plan.** Square lists Plus at "£20" "Per month", with a "Start 30-day free trial" button. Square describes Plus as being "For growing businesses that want to streamline work and billing needs with advanced, time-saving features." The page does not say whether the £20 includes or excludes VAT.

**Payment fees.** Square lists these processing rates under both the Free and Plus plans:

| How the customer pays | Square's published rate |
|---|---|
| Online card payment | 2.5% |
| In-person card payment | 1.75% |
| Card payment with Clearpay | 6% + 30p |
| Cash or cheque | No fee ("There are no fees for cheques or cash.") |
| Bank transfer | Not stated (see below) |

Square says you "Only pay for processing when you accept debit and credit card payments and Clearpay." About Clearpay, Square says: "You get paid right away, while they pay four interest-free instalments over six weeks."

**Bank transfers.** Square's UK invoices page says: "Customers can even use Apple Pay, Google Pay or ACH bank transfers to settle invoices right from their phone." ACH is a US bank payment system. The page does not explain whether customers can pay a Square invoice by UK bank transfer, and it does not give a fee for any bank transfer. If your customers usually pay by bank transfer, ask Square how this works in the UK before you rely on it.

**What else the page does not state.** The page does not say whether the card rates differ by card type or by where the card was issued, and it does not say whether the rates include VAT.

## SumUp Invoices: free plan limits and the SumUp Business Account requirement

Details in this section are from [SumUp Invoices](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/invoices/) and [SumUp Invoices pricing](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/invoices/pricing/), both checked 12 October 2026.

**Free plan.** SumUp's pricing page lists the Free plan at "£0" with an "Unlimited number of invoices and quotes". The product page says: "The Free plan is great for smaller businesses and includes unlimited invoices and quotes."

**The SumUp Business Account requirement.** The pricing page's comparison table has a "Receive bank transfers" row that sets out how bank-transfer payments reach you on each plan:

- **Free plan:** "Accept transfers to SumUp Business Account only". So on the Free plan, a customer's bank transfer goes into a SumUp Business Account first.
- **Plus plan:** "Accept direct transfers to any bank of your choice".

The same row also says: "You can transfer funds from your SumUp Business Account to any bank account afterwards." In other words, on the Free plan you can still move the money on to your own bank account; it simply arrives in a SumUp Business Account first, while Plus lets customers pay straight into the bank of your choice. Neither SumUp Invoices page sets out what a SumUp Business Account costs, what its terms are, or how long a transfer out of it takes.

**Plus plan.** SumUp's pricing page lists Invoices Plus at "£7" "per month, billed annually. Price excluding VAT." The product page puts it as "the equivalent of £7 + VAT per month" for the annual plan. Billed annually, that is £84 a year before VAT (12 × £7). The pricing page has a monthly billing option, but it shows no monthly-billing price for Plus.

**Payment fees.** SumUp's pricing page says:

| How the customer pays | SumUp's published fee |
|---|---|
| Online invoice payment | 2.5% ("The transaction fee is 2.5%, meaning for every £100 you invoice, we will take a £2.50 cut") |
| Bank transfer | No transaction fee |
| Cash | No transaction fee |

The no-fee rows come from the pricing page's statement: "There are no transaction fees for bank transfers or cash payments." The page also says: "We charge transaction fees for online invoice payments only." SumUp's product page says you can "Accept online card payments, bank transfers, cash, Google Pay and Apple Pay."

**Which plan the 2.5% fee applies to.** The pricing page's FAQ mentions the 2.5% fee in its description of the Free plan ("quotes with a 2.5% transaction fee for online invoice payments"). The page does not say whether Invoices Plus charges the same fee.

**What the pages do not state.** Neither SumUp page gives a fee for taking an in-person card payment against an invoice. Only the 2.5% fee for online invoice payments is published there. If you plan to take card payments face to face, see our guide to [taking card payments as a UK tradesperson](/guides/card-payments/) and check SumUp's own pages.

## Zoho Invoice: the free plan and its limits

All details in this section are from [Zoho Invoice pricing (UK)](https://www.zoho.com/uk/invoice/pricing/), checked 12 October 2026.

**Price.** Zoho's UK pricing page shows a price of "£0" and describes Zoho Invoice as "forever-free". It says: "We neither ask you for your credit card details while signing up nor collect any charges once you start using Zoho Invoice." No paid plan is listed on this page.

**Limits.** The page lists these limits:

- "Add up to two users."
- "Send up to 500 invoices per year."
- "Create a maximum of three projects."
- Invoices carry "Powered by Zoho Invoice" branding.

**Card payments.** The page's features list says "Accept payments via credit cards, debit cards & ACH." The page does not say whether taking card payments needs a separate payment gateway account, and it publishes no card processing fee. "Payment Gateways" appears only as a menu label. Because neither point is stated, this guide gives no card fee for Zoho Invoice. If card payments matter to you, ask Zoho how they work and what they cost before you rely on them.

## Side-by-side cost on a typical £500 job paid by card or bank transfer

This example uses a £500 invoice and only the rates each provider publishes on the pages above. It assumes the free plan, so the plan cost is £0. It does not add VAT to any fee, because none of the pages says whether VAT applies to its processing rates.

| How the customer pays £500 | Square (Free plan) | SumUp (Free plan) | Zoho Invoice (free) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan cost | £0 | £0 | £0 |
| Online card payment | 2.5% = £12.50 fee; you receive £487.50 | 2.5% = £12.50 fee; you receive £487.50 | Not stated |
| In-person card payment | 1.75% = £8.75 fee; you receive £491.25 | Not stated | Not stated |
| Card payment with Clearpay | 6% + 30p = £30.30 fee; you receive £469.70 | Not stated | Not stated |
| Bank transfer | Not stated (the page mentions only "ACH bank transfers", a US system, and gives no fee) | No transaction fee; £500 arrives in a SumUp Business Account, from which SumUp says you can transfer funds to any bank account | Not stated |
| Cash | No fee; you receive £500 | No fee; you receive £500 | Not stated |

How the figures are worked out:

- 2.5% of £500 = £12.50, leaving £487.50
- 1.75% of £500 = £8.75, leaving £491.25
- 6% of £500 = £30.00, plus 30p = £30.30, leaving £469.70

**If you choose a paid plan**, add its cost. Square Plus is listed at £20 per month (VAT basis not stated). SumUp Invoices Plus is listed at £7 per month billed annually, excluding VAT, which is £84 a year before VAT. Square's page shows the same processing rates under its Free and Plus plans. SumUp's pricing page mentions its 2.5% fee for online invoice payments in its description of the Free plan, and does not say whether Plus charges the same fee.

**What this means in practice:**

- If most of your customers pay online by card, Square's and SumUp's published online rates on their free plans are the same (2.5%).
- If you take card payments in person, Square publishes a rate (1.75%). SumUp's invoice pages do not.
- If customers often pay by bank transfer, SumUp publishes no transaction fee for bank transfers. On SumUp's Free plan, those payments arrive in a SumUp Business Account, and SumUp's pricing page says "You can transfer funds from your SumUp Business Account to any bank account afterwards." Invoices Plus accepts "direct transfers to any bank of your choice". SumUp's invoice pages do not state what a SumUp Business Account costs or its terms.
- Square's page mentions "ACH bank transfers", a US system, but does not say whether UK bank transfers are supported or what they cost. Ask Square before choosing it for bank-transfer payments.
- If you want an app with no stated subscription and will send no more than 500 invoices a year, Zoho Invoice fits on price, but its card payment costs are not published on its pricing page.

## When a job-management app makes more sense than an invoicing app

An invoicing app is built around one task: getting a bill to a customer and getting paid. If you also need to book jobs, schedule visits, keep customer records and track a job from quote to completion, a job-management app may suit you better, as these apps aim to cover those steps and the invoice in one place. Features and prices differ between products, so check each one against what you need.

Our guide to [job-management apps for UK trades](/guides/trade-job-apps/) compares options for one- and two-person businesses. If your main concern is keeping track of enquiries rather than invoices, see [keeping track of enquiries](/guides/enquiry-tracking/).
