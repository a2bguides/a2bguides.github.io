> **Last checked: 11 October 2026.** Prices and rules change; check the linked sources before relying on them.

This guide explains how a trade business that travels to customers can appear on Google Maps and Search without showing a home address, how Google's verification works, and how to start asking for reviews. Every rule below comes from Google's own Business Profile Help pages, which are listed in full under Sources. Where those pages do not say something, we say so.

## Who this is for

This guide is for UK sole traders and one- or two-person trade and service businesses, such as plumbers, electricians, decorators and cleaners, who:

- travel to customers' homes or premises to do the work
- usually run the business from a home address
- have no shop, workshop or office that customers visit.

Google's help pages are written for businesses worldwide. None of the pages used for this guide states a rule that applies only to the UK, and none says that any of its rules is different in the UK. The wording on Google's pages is its own (for example, "postal codes" rather than "postcodes", and "P.O. boxes"). Treat everything below as Google's general guidance and check the linked page before you act.

## Service-area profile or address profile: which one a trade business should set up

Google describes two kinds of business that go to customers:

- **Service-area business:** "A business that visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address." Google gives "cleaning services or plumbers" as examples ([Google: Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), checked 11 October 2026).
- **Hybrid business:** "A business that serves customers at its business address but also directly visits or delivers to them" (same page, checked 11 October 2026).

Google also says: "If your business doesn't have permanent on-site signage, it's not eligible as a storefront and should be listed as a service-area business" ([Google: Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), checked 11 October 2026).

For a tradesperson working from home with no customer-facing premises and no permanent signage, those stated facts point to a **service-area profile**. A hybrid set-up applies only if customers actually come to your business address. The same page notes that businesses selling products or services with an age requirement, such as alcohol or weapons, need a storefront, which is unlikely to affect most trades (checked 11 October 2026).

Google's guidelines add some limits that matter for home-based trades ([Google: Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), checked 11 October 2026):

- If you do not have a storefront with clear signage but travel to customers, "you're allowed one service-area Business Profile."
- "There should only be one profile per business," because more than one "can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search."
- A virtual office (a rented mailing address that you do not operate from) "isn't eligible for a Business Profile." For service-area businesses, Google says they "can't list a 'virtual' office unless that office is staffed during business hours."
- "P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable."

So a home-based trade should not try to use a rented mailing address or a PO box to avoid using the home address. The way to keep the home address private is to hide it, as described in the next section.

## Hiding your home address and setting up to 20 service areas

### Hiding the address

Google's guideline is direct: "If you're a service-area business, you should hide your business address from customers." It gives an example that fits this audience closely: "if you're a plumber and run your business from your residential address, clear the address from your Business Profile" ([Google: Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), checked 11 October 2026).

The service-area page says the same: "If you don't serve customers at your business address, remove your address from your Business Profile." If you are changing business type, Google's instruction is: "Remove your address and only enter your service area" ([Google: Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), checked 11 October 2026).

### Setting service areas

Key rules from [Google: Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481) (checked 11 October 2026):

- You can set **up to 20 service areas**, based on "the cities, postal codes, or other areas you serve." For a UK trade, that might mean the towns or postcodes you cover; check which suggestions Google offers as you type them in.
- You **cannot** set a service area as a radius around your base: "You can't set your service area as a radius distance around your business." If a radius was set up in the past, "you can't edit it."
- The overall area "shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based." Google's guidelines add that "For some businesses, larger service areas may be appropriate" ([Google: Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), checked 11 October 2026).
- Google asks you to "be as specific and accurate as possible."
- To remove all service areas, you must be a storefront business with your address shown, so a home-based service-area business will always have at least one service area.

### Steps to add or edit service areas

As set out by Google ([Google: Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), checked 11 October 2026):

1. Go to your Business Profile.
2. Select **Edit profile**, then **Location**.
3. Next to "Service area", select **Edit**.
4. Select a suggested service area or enter one.
5. Select **Save**.

Google says: "If approved, service area edits may take up to 48 hours to show on your Business Profile."

## Getting verified: the methods Google may offer and what to expect with video verification

### You do not choose the method

Google states: "Verification methods are automatically determined by Google and can't be changed." It advises you to "try the available options for your Business Profile," and notes that "In some cases, you may need to verify your business with more than one method" ([Google: Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), checked 11 October 2026).

The methods shown on that page are:

- phone or text
- email
- live video call
- postcard (shown under "Mail")
- video recording (shown as a link to a separate page, "Verify your business with a video recording").

Not every business will be offered every method. Google says: "Verification through postcard isn't available for all businesses" (same page, checked 11 October 2026).

### Video verification

Google's verification page says: "If your business is eligible for video verification, we recommend that you use this option" ([Google: Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), checked 11 October 2026).

That page does not explain what a video recording involves, what to show on camera, or how long it takes. Those details are on a separate Google page linked from the verification page, and this guide does not cover them. Read Google's video recording page from the verification page link before you start, so you know what Google asks to see.

### Postcard codes

If you are offered a postcard, Google's page says (checked 11 October 2026):

- "Most codes arrive within 14 days."
- "Verification codes expire after 30 days."
- While you wait: "Do not edit your business name, address, or category on your Business Profile. If you do, the code we sent you won't work."
- "Do not request a new code."
- "Google will never ask for your verification code." If someone contacts you asking for it, do not give it to them.

### How long review takes

Google states that "verification reviews take up to 5 business days," and adds that "On rare occasions, reviews may take longer" ([Google: Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), checked 11 October 2026).

## Asking customers for reviews with a Google review link or QR code

### Check that reviews apply to you

Google says: "Reviews are available only in certain countries or regions and for certain business categories" ([Google: Manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050), checked 11 October 2026). The page does not list which countries or categories. It is not stated there whether reviews are available for every UK trade category, so check your own profile.

### Creating a review link or QR code

Google lets you "create and share a link or QR code to request reviews." One important limit: "Currently, reviews QR codes can only be generated on a computer browser, not on mobile devices" ([Google: Create a Google link or QR code to request reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815), checked 11 October 2026). If you run the business from your phone, you will need a laptop or desktop computer for the QR code.

Steps, as Google shows them (same page, checked 11 October 2026):

1. Go to your Business Profile.
2. Select **Read Reviews**, then **Get more reviews**, then the Share icon.
3. Share the link or QR code directly, or use the other options shown.

- To copy the review link, select **Copy**.
- To save the QR code, right-click it and select **Save image as...**

Google says you can also send review requests "through email, WhatsApp message, or Facebook post." Its suggestions for encouraging customers include adding the link or code to receipts, thank-you emails and the end of a chat. For a trade business, receipts, invoices and follow-up messages after a job are the closest match; Google's suggestion to display the QR code "in your store" may not apply if you have no premises.

Customers "must be signed into a Google Account to leave a review" ([Google: Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), checked 11 October 2026).

### No incentives

Do not offer discounts, free work or anything else in return for a review. Google says: "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, to customers in exchange for reviews is considered fake engagement and is strictly prohibited." This "includes posting reviews, changing reviews, or removing negative reviews" ([Google: Create a Google link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815), checked 11 October 2026; see also [Google: Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), checked 11 October 2026).

Many marketing agencies publish advice on how to rank higher on Google Maps. The Google help pages listed in this guide do not give ranking advice, so this guide does not include any. Treat ranking tips from third parties as unverified unless Google's own pages support them.

## Replying to reviews and reporting ones that break Google's policies

All points in this section are from [Google: Manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050), checked 11 October 2026.

### Replying

- "Before you can reply to reviews, you must verify your business."
- Steps: go to your Business Profile, select **Read reviews**, select **Reply** next to the review, enter your reply in the dialogue box, then select **Reply**.
- Google checks replies to make sure "they follow Google's content policies." Replies "usually take up to 10 minutes to review, but sometimes a review can take up to 30 days."
- If a reply is not approved, "you'll be asked to edit it." If it is approved, "it will be publicly posted under the customer's review."
- "The person who wrote the review is notified when you reply."
- "Customers can still change their review after reading your reply." If they do, the date on the review changes to show the latest update.

Because the customer is notified and can still edit their review, a calm, factual reply is in your interest, but Google's page gives no rules on tone beyond its content policies.

### Reporting a review

Google says: "If you believe that a review violates Google's content policies, you can flag it." The page links to a separate article, "Learn how to report inappropriate reviews." This guide does not set out the reporting steps; follow that link from the Manage customer reviews page for Google's current instructions.

## Connecting your profile to your enquiry and job tracking

Once your profile is live, customers may contact you through it. Record each of those enquiries in the same place as your other work so none are missed and you can see which jobs came from Google.

Our existing guides cover this:

- [Set up a free enquiry logger](/guides/enquiry-logger/) – setting up a simple log for every new enquiry.
- [Keeping track of enquiries](/guides/enquiry-tracking/) – following each enquiry through to quote, job and follow-up.

A practical habit is to add "Google Business Profile" as a source option in your enquiry log. When you finish a job, the same record can remind you to send the review link or QR code described above.
