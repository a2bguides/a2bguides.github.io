Looking for Christmas gift ideas for a cold-water or wild swimmer? Most of us know someone who heads to the sea, a lake or the local lido in December and comes back looking pleased with themselves. Buying for them can feel tricky, but the most useful presents are not about the swim itself. They are about the minutes afterwards, when they are wet, cold and trying to get dressed. This guide sorts gift ideas around that routine, from warm layers and flasks to changing mats, then covers visibility kit, sizing pitfalls and lighter ideas for the swim group.

## What winter swimmers really need: warming up, not just swimming

The National Water Safety Forum says "open water in the UK can remain very cold all year round", and treats water of 15°C or below as dangerous for cold water shock ([National Water Safety Forum](https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/advice-and-information/open-water-swimming), checked 15 October 2026). The RNLI puts the average UK and Irish sea temperature at just 12°C ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), checked 15 October 2026).

That makes warming up afterwards a big part of the outing. The Outdoor Swimming Society's advice starts bluntly: "Get dressed! Don't dilly-dally on the shore, riverbank or the edge of your lido." It also explains that afterdrop "occurs when your core body temperature continues to cool after you're out" ([Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/warming-up-after-drop/), checked 15 October 2026).

So there is a simple test for any gift: does it help with getting dry, dressed and warm after a swim? If it does, it is likely to be welcome.

### A word on "challenge" gifts

It's tempting to buy something that celebrates toughness, such as a log book for minutes spent in the water. The Outdoor Swimming Society calls the "one minute per degree" rule a myth, saying it is "not actually true" because "every body is different", and that it has become "something to aspire to, and (worse) something to outdo". Its advice is to "drop the macho-minute-per-degree nonsense, and do it for you" ([Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/the-1-minute-per-degree-myth/), checked 15 October 2026; also summarised on its [cold-water category page](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/category/survive/cold/), checked 15 October 2026). Presents about comfort and company are a kinder choice than presents about endurance.

## After-swim warmth: robes, layers and flasks

**Layers.** The RNLI's list for once you're out is: "thermals, woolly jumpers, hats, scarves, and a warm coat" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), checked 15 October 2026). The Outdoor Swimming Society suggests "thermals, jumpers, insulated jackets, hat and gloves" ([Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/warming-up-after-drop/), checked 15 October 2026). Any of these makes a sensible present: a good woolly hat, a thermal top, a thick jumper, a scarf or warm gloves.

**Robes and big coats.** The RNLI's dipping page carries the line "Wrap up warm with big coats or a dry robe" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), checked 15 October 2026). A robe is a bigger present, so read the sizing notes below before buying one.

**Flasks.** The National Water Safety Forum suggests you "take a hot drink in a flask to help you warm up" ([National Water Safety Forum](https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/advice-and-information/open-water-swimming), checked 15 October 2026), and the RNLI's festive advice says to bring "a hot drink for when you come out of the water" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/december/15/rnli-offers-essential-safety-advice-for-cold-water-dippers-this-festive-period), checked 15 October 2026). The Outdoor Swimming Society notes that scientists say the small volume of a hot drink has no effect on core temperature, but that it still seems to help swimmers ([Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/warming-up-after-drop/), checked 15 October 2026). Either way, a sturdy flask or a good insulated mug fits neatly into the post-swim routine.

**What to skip: foil blankets.** Both the RNLI ("Don't use foil blankets – they don't work for swimmers") and the Outdoor Swimming Society ("Silver foil blankets don't help swimmers") are clear on this ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping); [Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/warming-up-after-drop/), both checked 15 October 2026).

## Neoprene and safety kit: gloves, socks, bright caps and tow floats

**Bright swim caps.** Swim England says "Wear a brightly coloured swim hat" ([Swim England](https://www.swimming.org/openwater/outdoor-swimming-safety-avoidable-tragedies/), checked 15 October 2026; also on its [open water safety page](https://www.swimming.org/openwater/open-water-safety/), checked 15 October 2026), and the RNLI suggests "a brightly coloured swim cap or beanie hat, so you're easy to spot in the water" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), checked 15 October 2026). A couple of caps in loud colours make an easy present.

**Tow floats and whistles.** Swim England's advice includes "Take a tow float" ([Swim England](https://www.swimming.org/openwater/outdoor-swimming-safety-avoidable-tragedies/), checked 15 October 2026). The RNLI pairs a bright cap "with a tow float to use in an emergency" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/december/15/rnli-offers-essential-safety-advice-for-cold-water-dippers-this-festive-period), checked 15 October 2026). The National Water Safety Forum suggests swimming with a whistle, or "a float with a whistle attached so you can call for help if you get into trouble" ([National Water Safety Forum](https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/advice-and-information/open-water-swimming), checked 15 October 2026).

Company matters as much as kit. The National Water Safety Forum says "it's safer to swim with a friend or an organised group", and the RNLI says "Never go cold water dipping alone" ([National Water Safety Forum](https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/advice-and-information/open-water-swimming); [RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), both checked 15 October 2026).

**Wetsuits, gloves and socks.** Neoprene gloves, socks and wetsuits can all make good presents. Swim England says wetsuits "help insulate against the cold and keep you buoyant in open water" ([Swim England](https://www.swimming.org/openwater/outdoor-swimming-safety-avoidable-tragedies/), checked 15 October 2026). Check what your swimmer actually wears before buying.

### Sizing when you're buying for someone else

Neoprene and robes are the hardest gifts to get right without the person there. This guide does not summarise any particular maker's sizing rules, so work from the size guide for the exact item you have in mind.

- **Check the guide for that item.** Look up the size guide for the specific robe or wetsuit on the maker's own website before choosing a size.
- **Find out the measurements it asks for.** If you can get them without spoiling the surprise, compare them with the chart rather than guessing.

Whatever you buy, keep the receipt. For gloves and socks, a gift card for a swim shop they already use is a perfectly good answer.

## Bags, mats and the getting-dressed problem

Getting changed on a beach or lakeside in winter is fiddly, so small things that make it easier are good gifts.

**A changing mat.** The RNLI suggests: "Use a changing mat while getting dressed to avoid losing more heat from your feet" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/water-safety/choose-your-activity/cold-water-dipping), checked 15 October 2026). The Outdoor Swimming Society likewise advises standing on something as you get changed, and says "Some winter swimmers take a thermos of warm water which they pour into a small tub to stand in while getting dressed" ([Outdoor Swimming Society](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/warming-up-after-drop/), checked 15 October 2026). A changing mat is a practical gift for most swimmers, and a small tub may suit someone who already uses, or wants to try, the warm-water approach.

**A bag that keeps things in order.** The National Water Safety Forum says to "make sure your towel and clothes are easily accessible" ([National Water Safety Forum](https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/advice-and-information/open-water-swimming), checked 15 October 2026). A waterproof bag with a separate section for wet kit, or a large tote they can pack in the order they'll need things, helps with that.

## Gifts for the swim group: jokes only dippers understand

Safety bodies encourage swimming together, and the RNLI advises "joining a group or organised activity so you can look out for each other" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/december/15/rnli-offers-essential-safety-advice-for-cold-water-dippers-this-festive-period), checked 15 October 2026). If you're buying for the whole group, think about things they can share: a big communal flask, a set of matching bright caps, or a framed group photo for whoever hosts the post-swim coffee.

In-jokes go down well too. The RNLI's festive safety advice put water temperatures in the UK at "around 6-10 degrees at this time of year" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/december/15/rnli-offers-essential-safety-advice-for-cold-water-dippers-this-festive-period), checked 15 October 2026), so the [Single Digits Swim Club 9° T-shirt](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4594744069) is a joke about the water temperature, not about how long anyone stays in. That keeps it on the right side of the minute-per-degree myth.

Festive dips have their own following. The RNLI says a festive cold-water dip is "becoming a popular activity at this time of year" and that for some it has become "a part of the annual tradition of Christmas and New Year celebrations" ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/december/15/rnli-offers-essential-safety-advice-for-cold-water-dippers-this-festive-period), checked 15 October 2026). For the friend whose festive dip falls on 26 December, the [Boxing Day Dipper sweatshirt](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595499423) has "BOXING DAY DIPPER 26 DEC" on the front.

## Stocking fillers and small presents

- **A bright swim cap or two**, in the loudest colours you can find.
- **A whistle** to clip to a float or bag.
- **A woolly hat**, scarf or warm gloves for afterwards.
- **An insulated mug** for the drink from the flask.
- **A sticker** for a flask, water bottle or kit bag, like the one mentioned above, or the [Boxing Day Dipper sticker](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4594691709) for a festive dipper.
- **A book or a local swim map** for planning new spots in spring.

Whatever you choose, the gifts most likely to be appreciated are the ones that fit into the cold, damp minutes after a swim.
