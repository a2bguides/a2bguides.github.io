Looking for a Christmas present for a beekeeper but don't know a brood box from a super? This guide covers gift ideas that are safe to buy even if you have never kept bees: what to give, what to ask about first, and what to leave to the beekeeper. Anyone who lives with a beekeeper, or has one in the family, knows how much of the year revolves around the hives. The British Beekeepers Association (BBKA) even suggests that December is the time to "write a Christmas list" of beekeeping kit ([BBKA, The Beekeeping Year](https://www.bbka.org.uk/the-beekeeping-year), checked 15 October 2026). If your beekeeper hasn't handed theirs over yet, read on. The guide is written mainly for buyers in the UK, with short notes for readers in the US.

## Why buying beekeeping kit as a gift is tricky (and what's safe)

Beekeeping kit is hard to buy blind. The BBKA's list of minimum equipment starts with "A suitable hive", and notes that "The most common type of hive in the UK is the National" ([BBKA, Becoming a Beekeeper](https://www.bbka.org.uk/becoming-a-beekeeper), checked 15 October 2026). Most common is not the same as universal, though. Which hive your beekeeper runs, which frames they use and which suit they like to wear are their own choices, and not things to guess at. When in doubt, the BBKA's general advice is to "Contact your local BBKA Association for advice" (same page), and the same principle works for gift-givers: the beekeeper, or a friend from their bee group, will know what they already have.

So for anything to do with hives, frames or protective clothing, there are two sensible routes:

- **Ask first.** Bring it up casually over dinner, or have a quiet word with someone they keep bees with.
- **Give a gift voucher.** Find out which beekeeping supplier they usually buy from and get a voucher there, so they can choose exactly what fits their set-up. Before buying, read the voucher's terms: how long it lasts, whether it is posted or emailed, and where it can be spent. If an e-voucher is sent by email, check whose address it goes to, so it doesn't land in their inbox before Christmas Day.

A voucher can feel less personal than a wrapped box. Pair it with something small and fun from the stocking-filler section below and it comes across very differently.

## Useful gifts for the winter months, when the hives are left alone

It helps to know what a beekeeper is actually doing around Christmas. The BBKA's month-by-month calendar for November says "It's hand off now until spring!" and "Leave the bees in peace!" Its list for December includes "Continue external checks especially after bad weather", "Heft to check stores and add fondant if required", "Clean equipment ready for next year" and "Review the season's successes and failures and consider next year's plan". January opens with "leave the bees alone!" and suggests beekeepers "Think about what equipment you may need for the new season and check prices and lead times with suppliers" ([BBKA, The Beekeeping Year](https://www.bbka.org.uk/the-beekeeping-year), checked 15 October 2026).

The BBKA's December 2023 apiary blog, by Karl Colyer, has a section headed "Leave the bees alone", urges beekeepers to "hold back the temptation to have a peep inside", and signs off with "Happy reading, planning and prepping for the next season!" ([BBKA, December in the Apiary 2023](https://www.bbka.org.uk/blog/december-in-the-apiary-2023), checked 15 October 2026).

In other words, the hives stay closed and winter is mostly for reading, cleaning and planning. Gifts that suit that rhythm include:

- **Something to plan with.** A good notebook or diary for hive records and next season's ideas fits neatly with the BBKA's suggestion to "consider next year's plan".
- **Help with the clean-up.** Sturdy storage boxes, labels, or an offer of a few hours of your own time in the shed all count.
- **Something for the wall or the shelf.** A bee-themed print or calendar keeps the hobby in view while the hives stay shut.
- **Reading material.** See the next section.

## Books, courses and association membership

The BBKA's calendar mentions reading in both November ("Read the books you haven't had time to open during the season") and January ("Take the opportunity to read some of the excellent beekeeping books which are available") ([BBKA, The Beekeeping Year](https://www.bbka.org.uk/the-beekeeping-year), checked 15 October 2026). That makes a beekeeping book one of the few kit-free gifts that fits the season perfectly. If you aren't sure what is already on their shelf, a bookshop voucher does the same job.

**Local association membership** is another thoughtful option. The BBKA's Find Beekeeping Near You page says "There are over 270 beekeeping area associations and branches who serve their local community with support and education", and "The majority offer beekeeping experiences and training courses, support local events and sell honey." It has an interactive map for finding a local group. For Scotland, the page points to the Scottish Beekeepers' Association, and for Wales to the Welsh Beekeepers' Association ([BBKA, Find Beekeeping Near You](https://www.bbka.org.uk/find-beekeeping-near-you), checked 15 October 2026).

Membership options vary. The BBKA's membership page says "Partner, Junior, Country and School memberships are also available", but these are only available through BBKA affiliated associations, not as direct memberships. It also says that fees through a local association "are often less than those of Direct Membership" ([BBKA, Become a member of the BBKA](https://www.bbka.org.uk/become-a-member-of-the-bbka), checked 15 October 2026). Prices are set locally, so contact the local association to ask what membership costs and whether someone else can pay for it. If your beekeeper is already a member, they will know when their renewal is due, and paying it for them could be a welcome surprise.

**For US readers:** the American Beekeeping Federation lists a "Small Scale Member" level, "Recommended for Hobbyists, Scientists and Students", at $60 a year ([ABF, Join or Renew](https://abfnet.org/join-or-renew-now/), checked 15 October 2026).

## Consumables and wearables every beekeeper gets through

Things that get used up are easier to give than big equipment, but beekeepers have their own preferences here too, so ask what they use before buying. The BBKA's minimum equipment list includes protective clothing, a smoker with fuel and a hive tool ([BBKA, Becoming a Beekeeper](https://www.bbka.org.uk/becoming-a-beekeeper), checked 15 October 2026), which suggests a few natural ideas:

- **Gloves.** The BBKA's list mentions "disposable 'Marigold' type gloves that cover the wrists" (same page). Ask what size and type they prefer before buying a box.
- **A spare hive tool.** They may already have a favourite style, so ask which one they like or put a voucher towards it.
- **Fondant.** The BBKA's December list includes "Heft to check stores and add fondant if required" ([BBKA, The Beekeeping Year](https://www.bbka.org.uk/the-beekeeping-year), checked 15 October 2026). Whether their colonies need it is the beekeeper's call, so this one is best bought on request, in the kind they already use.
- **Smoker fuel.** Again, ask what they burn first, and buy more of the same.

Suits and veils are best left to the beekeeper, who will know what they like to wear. A voucher lets them pick exactly what they want.

## Stocking fillers and gifts that make beekeepers laugh

Not every present has to be practical. Bee-themed socks, tea towels or a jar of honey all make cheerful smaller gifts. Since the BBKA says most local associations "sell honey" ([BBKA, Find Beekeeping Near You](https://www.bbka.org.uk/find-beekeeping-near-you), checked 15 October 2026), a jar from a neighbouring group could be a nice touch, though a jar from their own hives may be the one thing they don't need.

## Gifts for someone who wants to start beekeeping

If the person on your list is still at the "thinking about it" stage, kit is the wrong place to start. The BBKA says "If you are new to beekeeping you will need the help and advice of your local beekeeping association", and that most associations offer "beekeeping experiences and training courses" ([BBKA, Find Beekeeping Near You](https://www.bbka.org.uk/find-beekeeping-near-you), checked 15 October 2026). Its Becoming a Beekeeper page describes a local association as a place "where you will be given advice, tuition and support" ([BBKA, Becoming a Beekeeper](https://www.bbka.org.uk/becoming-a-beekeeper), checked 15 October 2026).

That makes a beginners' course or a place on a local beekeeping experience a natural present. Dates and prices are set by each association, so find the nearest one on the BBKA map and ask them directly. On equipment, the Becoming a Beekeeper page notes that second-hand kit can be bought, "but always be mindful of the potential for introducing diseases" (same page). That is another reason to let a new beekeeper choose their kit with their association's help rather than buying a hive as a surprise. A notebook for course notes, a beekeeping book or one of the stocking fillers above makes a good companion to a course place.

**For US readers:** the American Beekeeping Federation's beginner toolkit suggests "Join the ABF and a local bee club" and "Make friends with another local beekeeper" ([ABF, Beginning Beekeeping Toolkit](https://abfnet.org/beginning-beekeeping-toolkit/), checked 15 October 2026). A year's club membership or the cost of a local class could make a good gift there too; ask the club directly about prices.
