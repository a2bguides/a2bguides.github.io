Buying for someone who lives on, or spends every spare weekend aboard, a narrowboat is a particular puzzle. Every cupboard is already spoken for, and anything bulky will end up back at your house by February. The good news is that boaters are easy to please once you think the way they do: small, useful, and ideally something that gets used up.

## The golden rule: small, useful or consumable

Space on a narrowboat is tight, so our own rule of thumb is to lean towards three kinds of present:

- **Consumables** – things that get eaten, drunk, burned or used up, so they leave no trace by spring.
- **Small, genuinely useful items** – something that earns its place in a drawer.
- **Things to wear** – clothing lives on the boater rather than in a cupboard, and a replacement for a worn-out favourite takes no extra room at all.

That is simply our suggestion rather than anyone's official advice. If in doubt, ask a quiet question about what they keep running out of. Most boaters have a list.

## Winter on the cut: gifts for warmth, damp and dark evenings

Plenty of boaters stay out all winter, whether they are continuous cruisers or tied up on a winter mooring. The Trust's winter moorings for 2026/27 run "for up to four months from" 1 November 2026 "until" 28 February 2027, giving boaters without a permanent mooring "a chance to moor up for the winter months" ([Canal & River Trust, Winter moorings](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/moorings/winter-moorings), checked 17 October 2026). That is a long stretch of short days, so winter-comfort gifts tend to go down well.

**Stove fuel and kindling.** The Boat Safety Scheme notes that solid fuel stoves make "thousands of boats on UK inland waterways very cosy" ([Boat Safety Scheme, Solid Fuel Stoves](https://www.boatsafetyscheme.org/stay-safe-advice/solid-fuel-stoves/), checked 17 October 2026). If the boater in your life has a stove, fuel is about as consumable as a gift gets. Writing about winter fuel, the Canal & River Trust says that "for the majority storage is limited, especially if you are cruising", so boaters "rely on the services of fuel boats and other solid fuel suppliers" ([Canal & River Trust, Keeping stocked up and warm](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/boating-news-and-views/boating-blogs/how-to-survive-your-first-winter/keeping-stocked-up-and-warm), checked 17 October 2026). For wood, the BSS advises: "For the best wood fuel choices look for the approved Ready to Burn certification", and stresses that "It is critical to your safety that wood fuel is kept in a dry, well-ventilated area" ([Boat Safety Scheme, Wood fuel wisdom](https://www.boatsafetyscheme.org/stay-safe-advice/solid-fuel-stoves/wood-fuel-wisdom/), checked 17 October 2026). Offering to cover their next fuel order could be a thoughtful alternative to a parcel, and a bag of kindling makes a handy add-on.

**Cleaning stove glass.** Stove-owning boaters spend a lot of evenings looking at the fire, and a sooty glass door spoils the view. A cleaner made for stove glass is a small, practical stocking filler. Leave choosing methods to them, and point anyone wanting stove guidance to the [BSS solid fuel stoves page](https://www.boatsafetyscheme.org/stay-safe-advice/solid-fuel-stoves/), which advises boaters to "ask a professional for advice or to fix any problems" (checked 17 October 2026).

**Damp and condensation.** The Trust says that "Condensation can be a big issue on boats during the winter", and that "Quite a few boaters recommend a window vacuum cleaner such as the one made by Karcher" for clearing window condensation each morning ([Canal & River Trust, Preparing yourself and your boat](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/boating-news-and-views/boating-blogs/how-to-survive-your-first-winter/preparing-yourself-and-your-boat), checked 17 October 2026). A window vacuum is compact and could make a sensible gift. The same page mentions that with "a decent battery bank and the means of charging those batteries" a boater "could consider a small 12v dehumidifier", so check their power set-up before buying one. The Trust is also clear that "The key thing is to make sure you don't block up any ventilation grilles" (same page, checked 17 October 2026).

**Warm things to wear.** The Trust recommends "proper winter and weatherproof clothing for those long trudges down muddy, slippery towpaths and steering in a rain storm" ([Canal & River Trust](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/boating-news-and-views/boating-blogs/how-to-survive-your-first-winter/preparing-yourself-and-your-boat), checked 17 October 2026), and for winter cruising it advises: "Wrap up warm, insulate your feet against the cold deck and take shorter turns at the helm" ([Canal & River Trust, Eight tips when winter cruising](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/go-boating/a-guide-to-boating/winter-cruising), checked 17 October 2026). As gift ideas, thick socks, a good hat, gloves the steerer can still work a windlass in, or a fresh pair of wellies are all things a boater is likely to wear out.

**Dark evenings.** Long nights moored up call for small comforts: a paperback, a pack of cards, good tea or coffee, or a box of treats from a local producer.

## Safety gifts that are worth giving, such as a certified CO alarm

A carbon monoxide alarm is not the most romantic present, but it is one of the most meaningful. The Boat Safety Scheme warns that "CO cannot be seen, tasted, smelt or felt - it is the silent killer", and that "Each year boaters die or are made seriously ill from carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning". Its advice is to "Install at least one certified CO alarm (BS EN 50291-2), test it routinely and never remove the batteries" ([Boat Safety Scheme, Carbon Monoxide (CO)](https://www.boatsafetyscheme.org/stay-safe-advice/carbon-monoxide-co/), checked 17 October 2026).

So if you buy one, look for that standard, BS EN 50291-2, on the packaging. On placement, the BSS suggests testing "that you can hear an alarm from any position in the boat (or buy an additional alarm)" and following "the alarm manufacturer's installation instructions" ([Boat Safety Scheme, Placing CO Alarms](https://www.boatsafetyscheme.org/stay-safe-advice/carbon-monoxide-co/placing-co-alarms/), checked 17 October 2026). With that in mind, an additional alarm could be a welcome gift even for someone who already has one.

## Maps, guides and the IWA for planning next season's cruise

Winter is when boaters plan where to go once the stoppages end, and a new guide is a classic present.

The best-known printed canal guides come in series that cover the network region by region, including the Nicholson Guides and the Pearson Canal Companions. The Birmingham Canal Navigations Society, which says of its resources page "We are not an online shop", lists the Nicholson Guide "Birmingham & The Heart of England" as "Another classic guide to all the BCN canals", alongside a Pearson Canal Companion described as "A new and updated edition published 2019" ([BCN Society, BCN resources for boaters](https://bcnsociety.com/bcn-resources-for-boaters/), checked 18 October 2026).

A fold-out map of Britain's inland waterways is another option, for the wall or the chart table. Prices and delivery terms for guides and maps are not given here, because they vary between sellers; check them before buying and order in good time for Christmas.

A tip: find out which waterways they are planning to cruise, or check which guides are already on board, before choosing one.

**IWA membership.** The Inland Waterways Association (IWA) describes itself as "the only independent, national charity campaigning for Britain's canals and rivers", says it aims to "Support the restoration of disused waterways, and bring them back to life", and gives its registered charity number as 212342 ([IWA, About us](https://waterways.org.uk/about-us), checked 18 October 2026). Membership prices and benefits are not listed here, so check the current terms with the IWA directly. For a boater who cares about the network, and about seeing lost canals brought back into use, membership is worth mentioning to them as something they could take up themselves.

## Stocking fillers for hire-boat regulars

Friends who hire a boat once or twice a year have no storage problem at home, but what they take aboard still needs to fit in a holdall. Ideas include:

- A pocket-sized guide or a fold-out waterways map for planning their next route.
- A waterproof phone pouch or a small dry bag for walks along the towpath.
- A head torch for finding things in the cratch after dark.
- A pair of good gloves for working locks.
- A tin of biscuits or a bag of fudge for the crew.

For anyone new to hiring, the Canal & River Trust's Boater's Handbook is well worth sending them a link to. The Trust says it "has everything you need to know about safely cruising our canals and rivers", whether you are "taking your first boating trip" or need "a little refresh", and it can be downloaded as a PDF from the [Boaters' handbook page](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/enjoy-the-waterways/boating/go-boating/a-guide-to-boating/boaters-handbook) (checked 17 October 2026).
