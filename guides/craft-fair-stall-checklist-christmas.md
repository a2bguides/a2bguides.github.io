Booked a stall at a Christmas craft fair or market and wondering what to take? Fair season comes round fast: one week the diary's empty, the next there are three Saturdays booked and a kitchen table covered in half-finished stock. This guide covers what to sort in the weeks before (organiser rules, insurance, pricing and bags), a printable stall kit checklist, display ideas that make people stop, how to stay warm at an outdoor Christmas market, where to find UK safety guidance for toys and candles, and gift ideas for the maker who does every fair going.

## Six Weeks Out: Booking, Stock and Pricing

**Read the organiser's rules before you pay.** Every event sets its own requirements. For example, Ripley Town Council's form for its Christmas event on Sunday 22 November 2026 asks stallholders to upload a copy of their public liability insurance with the application and to bring their own gazebo (no bigger than 3m x 3m) with weights on each leg. It also says gazebos, tables, chairs and market stalls will not be provided ([Ripley Town Council](https://www.ripleytowncouncil.gov.uk/2026-events/ripley-christmas-events-2026/christmas-2026-stall-application-form/), checked 18 October 2026). That's one organiser's event, not a general rule, but it shows why it's worth checking what's included before you load the car.

**Sort your insurance.** Hinckley & Bosworth Borough Council asks for evidence of up-to-date public liability insurance with a stall application for its market, and advises having a risk assessment ready too ([Hinckley & Bosworth Borough Council](https://www.hinckley-bosworth.gov.uk/info/200205/market_and_street_trading_licences/206/getting_a_stall_on_hinckley_market/2), checked 18 October 2026). The same page points to the National Market Traders Federation, saying membership is not compulsory but that traders who don't join will need to get cover elsewhere. The NMTF says its membership includes cover for public, product and employer's liability claims, underwritten by Aviva, with a limit of £10 million ([NMTF](https://www.nmtf.co.uk/), checked 18 October 2026). Whichever route you take, check with the insurer that the cover suits craft fairs and that it matches what your organiser asks for.

**Know whether you're trading.** GOV.UK says you're likely to be trading if you sell regularly to make a profit, and that people who only sell items occasionally should check whether they need to tell HMRC about the income. If you're not sure, it says to contact HMRC for advice ([GOV.UK: Working for yourself](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself), checked 18 October 2026).

**Price everything early.** For traders selling goods to consumers in England, Scotland and Wales, Business Companion says price information must be clearly legible, unambiguous and easily identifiable. It must be shown on the goods themselves or on a ticket or notice near them, and must include VAT and any other compulsory charges or taxes ([Business Companion: Providing price information](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/pricing-and-payment/providing-price-information), checked 18 October 2026). Pricing at the kitchen table in October is far less stressful than scribbling tags in a cold car park.

**Think about bags.** In England, retailers of any size must charge at least 10p (including VAT) for single-use carrier bags that are new, plastic, 70 microns thick or less, and have handles, an opening and no seal; some bags are exempt ([GOV.UK: Carrier bag charges](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/carrier-bag-charges-retailers-responsibilities), checked 18 October 2026). That guidance applies to England, so makers elsewhere should check their own local rules.

## The Stall Kit Checklist

Print this, stick it inside the lid of your main crate, and tick things off as you pack.

| Done | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Table, plus gazebo and weights if outdoors | Check whether the organiser supplies these. |
| ☐ | Table cover | Floor-length hides the boxes underneath. |
| ☐ | Risers, crates or shelves | To lift stock off a flat table. |
| ☐ | Price labels, tags and a pen | Plus spare blank tags. |
| ☐ | Sign saying what you make | Big enough to read from the aisle. |
| ☐ | Cash float | Coins and small notes. |
| ☐ | Card reader, fully charged | And its charging cable. |
| ☐ | Backup payment option | A second reader, a power bank, or a phone set up to take payments. |
| ☐ | Bags, tissue and wrapping | See the bag note above. |
| ☐ | Cable ties, tape, pegs and scissors | For signs, bunting and lights. |
| ☐ | Fairy lights or battery lighting | For late-afternoon and evening markets. |
| ☐ | Folding chair | Check whether one is provided. |
| ☐ | Flask and snacks | Something easy to eat between customers. |
| ☐ | Thermal layers, hat and mittens | For outdoor or chilly indoor events. |
| ☐ | Hand warmers | A few spares in the cash bag. |
| ☐ | Business cards or a QR code | So people can find you later. |
| ☐ | Insurance documents and organiser info | Somewhere easy to reach. |

On lighting, Ripley Town Council says part of its event will be in darkness and recommends that stallholders bring fairy lights or lighting (see the Ripley source above).

On card readers: halls and market squares can have patchy signal, so it helps to know how your reader behaves offline. Square's UK help page says offline payments must be uploaded within 72 hours of the start of the offline session, that you won't receive notifications for declined payments while offline, and that you're responsible for any expired, declined or disputed payments taken that way ([Square: Process offline payments](https://squareup.com/help/gb/en/article/7777-process-card-payments-with-offline-mode), checked 18 October 2026). SumUp's UK payment terms describe offline transactions as a backup solution, processed without immediate authorisation from the cardholder's bank, with the risk of declined or fraudulent transactions falling on the seller. They also say offline transactions can't be refunded during the offline session, only once the connection is back ([SumUp Payment Terms](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/terms/payment/), checked 18 October 2026). A cash float is still worth packing.

## Display Ideas That Make People Stop

**Build some height.** A flat table with everything at one level is easy to walk past. Crates, cake stands, tiered shelves, a small ladder or a pegboard at the back bring things nearer eye level. Put showpieces up high and small, affordable pieces at the front where people can pick them up.

**Make prices easy to see.** Not everyone likes to ask, so clear tags or a neat price board help browsers decide. As covered above, clear pricing is also a legal requirement for traders selling goods to consumers in England, Scotland and Wales.

**Put up a sign that says what you make.** A shop name says who you are; a short line such as "Hand-thrown mugs and planters" tells people what you sell before they reach the table. Hang it high at the back so it can be read over the crowd.

**Group by colour or price.** A "stocking fillers under a fiver" corner gives gift-buyers an easy place to start.

**Leave room to browse.** Keep back-up stock under the table and top up as you sell, rather than cramming everything out at once.

**Show it in use.** Wear the hat, drink from the mug, hang the wreath on the gazebo frame.

## Staying Warm and Sane at an Outdoor Christmas Market

The US National Weather Service's advice for extreme cold says to dress in layers and wear a warm hat, and that mittens are better than gloves. It also says to keep your skin dry, to eat hot foods and drink warm drinks several times during the day, and to avoid caffeine, alcohol and cigarettes ([National Weather Service: During Extremely Cold Weather](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-during), checked 18 October 2026). Its cold-weather kit list includes warmers for body, hands and feet, and protein-rich foods such as nuts and energy bars ([National Weather Service: Prepare for Cold Weather](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-before), checked 18 October 2026). That advice was written for extreme cold rather than markets, but it translates into a sensible bag: thermal layers, a hat, mittens, hand warmers, a flask of something warm and a stash of snacks.

For the "sane" part: a folding chair, a friend who'll mind the stall while you take a break, and a pre-written "back in five minutes" sign.

## Product Safety Basics for Handmade Goods

This section points to official guidance; it isn't legal advice. If you're unsure of your responsibilities, Business Companion suggests starting with your local Trading Standards service.

Business Companion, from the Chartered Trading Standards Institute, covers Trading Standards law for England, Scotland and Wales. It says goods intended for consumers, or reasonably likely to be used by them, must be safe and comply when they are placed on the market. Selling them, or even giving them away as a prize or gift, counts as supply, and anyone involved in supplying goods to consumers is responsible for the safety of what they supply ([Business Companion: Product safety](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/product-safety), checked 18 October 2026).

**Toys.** Business Companion defines toys as anything designed or intended, whether or not exclusively, for use in play by children under 14. All new toys supplied in the course of a business must carry certain markings, starting with the name and address of the manufacturer, and some toys must come with warnings and instructions. It says that if you make toys as well as sell them, there are many more requirements than its guide can cover, and asks makers to contact their local Trading Standards service for advice ([Business Companion: Toys](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/product-safety/toys), checked 18 October 2026).

**Candles and diffusers.** Business Companion says there are specific safety requirements for candles, diffusers, room sprays, fragrant aromatherapy oil heaters and lamps, and that candles need to be assessed for safety before being sold to consumers. It names BS EN 15494 for warning labels on indoor candles, alongside BS EN 15426 (sooting) and BS EN 15493 (fire safety). It also notes that a number of common fragrances are classified as sensitisers, which can bring labelling requirements depending on how much is used, while unscented candles often need no labelling at all ([Business Companion: Candles, diffusers, oil heaters, etc](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/product-safety/candles-diffusers-oil-heaters-etc), checked 18 October 2026). The full guide is worth reading before your first fair.

Business Companion's guidance is for England, Scotland and Wales, so makers in Northern Ireland or the US should check the rules where they trade.

## Gifts for the Maker Who Does Every Fair

Buying for someone who spends every December weekend behind a trestle table? These ideas should get plenty of use.

- **Storage.** Stackable clear crates, a divided box for small pieces, or a folding trolley for the trek from car park to hall.
- **A good card reader, or a backup.** Ask what they already use first, as most sellers like to stick with one system. A power bank is a handy add-on.
- **Display props.** A tiered stand, wooden crates, a chalkboard sign, a mirror for jewellery stalls, or battery fairy lights.
- **Warm kit.** Thermal socks, mittens, a big flask or a multipack of hand warmers make good stocking fillers.
- **A custom stamp or sticker roll** with their logo, for bags and packaging.
- **Something that answers the question for them.** Every maker hears "Did you make all this yourself?" more times than they can count. Our "Yes, it took ages" design answers that and the follow-up in one go. It comes as a [t-shirt](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595800664) to wear behind the stall, a [sweatshirt](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595815690) for colder days, a [tote bag](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595815760) for carrying supplies, a [mug](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4598587496) for the workshop, or a [sticker](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595779215) for a crate or cash box.
- **A day off.** An offer to mind the stall for an afternoon, or a lift there and back with the heavy crates.
