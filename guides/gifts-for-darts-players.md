Looking for Christmas gifts for a darts player? It looks easy until you're standing in front of a wall of barrels, flights and stems with no idea what they actually throw. The good news is that the presents a league regular welcomes most are rarely the darts themselves. This guide covers what to buy instead: kit for practice at home, gear for league night, a few jokes only a player will get, stocking fillers, and treats for the weeks when the World Championship is on the telly.

## Why you shouldn't buy them a set of darts (and what to buy instead)

Darts are about as personal as kit gets. A player settles on a weight, a barrel shape and a grip that suit their throw, and anything different can feel wrong in the hand. Wikipedia's article on darts notes that barrels "come in a variety of weights" and in "three basic shapes: cylindrical, ton, or torpedo", and says that "the choice of barrel, shaft, and flight will depend a great deal on the individual player's throwing style" [4]. The World Darts Federation's playing rules set only limits, not a recommended weight: darts must not exceed 30.5 cm in length or weigh more than 50 grams [2].

So unless they've told you the exact set they want, it's kinder to let them choose. A few ideas that work instead:

- **Cash towards a set.** Pair it with a card promising a trip to a darts shop so they can pick their own.
- **Something for the board at home** (see below), which everyone in the house benefits from.
- **Accessories that don't depend on their exact set**, such as a case, a scoreboard or a decent light for the board.
- **Something that makes them laugh**, because a darts joke on a mug lasts longer than most socks.

If you do want to buy flights or stems, have a quiet look at what's in their case first, or ask a teammate.

## Gifts for practice nights at home: boards, surrounds, lighting and practice aids

Plenty of league players practise on a board at home, and a better setup is a gift the whole household notices.

**Getting the board at the right height.** If you're helping put up a new board, the official figures are worth knowing. The WDF playing rules say the height from the floor to "a horizontal line through the centre of the 'Bull' shall measure 1.73 metres" (Rule 6.01), the throwing distance is "2.37 metres" measured "from the back of the Oche" to the face of the board (Rule 6.02), and the diagonal from the centre of the bull to the back of the oche at floor level "shall measure 2.93 metres" (Rule 6.03) [2]. The WDF gives these in metres only. If you'd rather work in feet and inches, Wikipedia's article on darts gives the height as 5 ft 8 in and the throwing distance as 7 ft 9 in, rounded to the nearest inch [4]; the WDF's metric figures are the official ones.

**A fresh board.** Boards wear, especially around the 20 and its neighbours. If their board has a pitted middle and darts keep bouncing out, a new sisal board is a very practical present. The WDF specifies sisal boards for its organised play [2].

**A surround to protect the wall.** A surround is the ring that sits around the board. Check which board they have, as some surrounds are made to fit particular models.

**Fixing and lighting.** A board light makes a thoughtful upgrade, but check how it attaches before buying, and follow the board maker's and the light maker's own fitting instructions.

**Practice aids.** Other ideas for home include an oche marker or throw-line mat for the floor, a wipe-clean scoreboard to hang beside the board, or a book of practice routines and checkout charts.

## Gifts for league night: cases, flights, stems and a spare set of shafts

League night means carrying darts to the pub, so kit that travels well always comes in handy.

- **A darts case or wallet.** A sturdy case keeps darts, spare flights and stems together in a jacket pocket or bag. Look for one with room for a spare set.
- **Spare flights and stems.** A pack of spares in the case makes a handy little gift, so nobody has to borrow from a teammate mid-match. Match the shape and fitting to what they already use; if you can't check, a small mixed pack is safer.
- **A flight tool or protectors.** Small accessories like these are cheap, useful and easy to tuck into a case.
- **Team kit.** If their team has a name, matching shirts for the whole side make a fun group present from the captain or a partner.

## Darts jokes only a player gets: bed and breakfast, the 26 and the wrong side of the 20

Every regular knows the groan. You aim for treble 20 and land a single 20, a 5 and a 1, either side of the 20. That's 26, known on the oche as "bed and breakfast". Wikipedia's glossary of darts defines it as "a score of 26, made up of a single-5, single-20, single-1 in a game of x01", explaining that players aiming for the 20 "will often accidentally hit the 1 and the 5 sectors, which are located on either side of the 20" [5]. Wikipedia, which is not an official darts source, also says the name comes from the old price of a night's bed and breakfast: two shillings and sixpence, or "two and six" [5]. That is Wikipedia's explanation rather than one from a darts body, so treat it as a good pub story rather than settled history.

## Watching the World Championship at Christmas: gifts for the armchair fan

For many players, Christmas means the World Championship. The PDC's FAQ page gives the dates of the 2026/27 Paddy Power World Darts Championship at Alexandra Palace as 11 December 2026 to 3 January 2027 [3]. Its schedule lists no sessions on 24, 25 or 26 December, with play resuming on Sunday 27 December, and the final on Sunday 3 January [3]. Sky Sports describes this as "the usual Christmas break between December 24-27" [6].

Gift ideas for the armchair fan:

- **A cosy night in**: a blanket, a big mug and their favourite snacks for the evening sessions.
- **Tickets**, if you can get them and they'd enjoy the atmosphere at Alexandra Palace; check the PDC's own ticket information for details.
- **A darts book**, such as a player biography or a history of the game.
- **A home scoreboard** so they can play along with the checkouts between legs.

## Small stocking fillers

Small, inexpensive extras are often the gifts a player uses most. A few suggestions:

- A pack of flights or stems in the style they already use
- A [Bed and Breakfast Again sticker](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595800640) for a darts case or laptop
- Chalk and a cloth for a chalk scoreboard, or pens for a wipe-clean one
- A checkout chart for the wall by the board
- A keyring or small case for spare flights
- A darts-themed Christmas card signed by the whole team
