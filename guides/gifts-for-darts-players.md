Buying for someone who plays darts looks easy until you're standing in front of a wall of barrels, flights and stems with no idea what they actually throw. The good news is that the most welcome presents for a league regular are rarely the darts themselves. This guide covers what to buy instead, from the home practice setup to league-night kit, a few jokes only a player will get, and treats for the weeks when the World Championship is on the telly.

## Why you shouldn't buy them a set of darts (and what to buy instead)

Darts are about as personal as kit gets. A player settles on a weight, a barrel shape and a grip that suits their throw, and anything different can feel wrong in the hand. Target Darts describes choosing darts as "a personal journey that requires some experimentation", advises players to "experiment with different weights to find the one that feels comfortable and suits your throwing style", and says "the shape of the barrel you should use is entirely down to how you would grip a dart" [6]. It also points out that "many dart shops offer the opportunity to try out different darts before making a purchase" [6]. The World Darts Federation's playing rules only set limits, not a recommended weight: darts must not exceed 30.5 cm in length or weigh more than 50 grams [2].

So unless they've told you the exact set they want, it's kinder to let them choose. A few ideas that work instead:

- **Cash towards a set.** Pair it with a card promising a trip to a darts shop where they can try a few before deciding.
- **Something for the board at home** (see below), which everyone in the house benefits from.
- **Accessories that don't depend on their exact set**, such as a case, a scoreboard or a decent light for the board.
- **Something that makes them laugh**, because a darts joke on a mug lasts longer than most socks.

If you do want to buy flights or stems, have a quiet look at what's in their case first, or ask a teammate.

## Gifts for practice nights at home: boards, surrounds, lighting and practice aids

Plenty of league players practise on a board at home, and a better setup is a gift the whole household notices.

**Getting the board at the right height.** If you're helping put up a new board, the official figures are worth knowing. The WDF playing rules say the height from the floor to "a horizontal line through the centre of the 'Bull' shall measure 1.73 metres" (Rule 6.01), the throwing distance is "2.37 metres" measured "from the back of the Oche" to the face of the board (Rule 6.02), and the diagonal from the centre of the bull to the back of the oche at floor level "shall measure 2.93 metres" (Rule 6.03) [2]. The WDF gives these in metres only. If you'd rather work in feet and inches, Winmau's setup guide gives the same three measurements as 5 feet 8 inches, 7 feet 9¼ inches and 9 feet 7½ inches, though it measures the throw to the front edge of the oche rather than the back [4].

**A fresh board.** Boards wear, especially around the 20 and its neighbours. Winmau recommends rotating a board "at regular intervals", saying that regular rotation "spreads impact across the entire playing surface, helping to prevent premature wear in high-use segments and extending the board's usable life" [4]. On its Blade 6 board, that means removing the number ring and turning the board "by a few segments" [5]. Winmau also notes that wear on the 25 and bull "cannot be protected by rotating the dartboard" [5]. As for how long a board lasts, Winmau calls that "an almost impossible question to answer" without knowing the setup, the player's throw and how often it's used [5]. If their board has a pitted middle and darts keep bouncing out, a new sisal board is a very practical present. The WDF specifies sisal boards for its organised play [2].

**A surround to protect the wall.** A surround is the ring that sits around the board. Target Darts says a surround "goes around your dartboard to protect your walls and surrounding area from stray darts" and that surrounds "offer a quieter game by absorbing the sound of darts hitting the dartboard" [7]. Prices vary by brand; as one example, Winmau listed regular prices of £39.99 to £47.99 for its surrounds on 16 October 2026 [8]. Check which board they have, as some surrounds are made to fit particular models.

**Fixing and lighting.** Board lights make a thoughtful upgrade, but check how a light attaches before buying. Winmau's Blade 6 setup instructions say the centre screw on the back of a Blade 6 board is designed to take the weight of the board and a traditional surround, but that heavier items "such as some lighting systems" may add excessive weight "and cause the board to fall from the wall potentially causing injury" [5]. That warning is Winmau's, about its Blade 6; for any other board or light, follow that maker's own fitting instructions. On lighting generally, Target notes that "the lighting in the room can affect your ability to see the dartboard clearly" [7].

**Practice aids.** Other ideas for home include an oche marker or throw-line mat for the floor, a wipe-clean scoreboard to hang beside the board, or a book of practice routines and checkout charts.

## Gifts for league night: cases, flights, stems and a spare set of shafts

League night means carrying darts to the pub, so kit that travels well is always handy.

- **A darts case or wallet.** A sturdy case keeps darts, spare flights and stems together in a jacket pocket or bag. Look for one with room for a spare set.
- **Spare flights and stems.** A pack of spares in the case makes a handy little gift, so nobody has to borrow from a teammate mid-match. Match the shape and fitting to what they already use; if you can't check, a small mixed pack is safer.
- **A flight tool or protectors.** Small accessories like these are cheap, useful and easy to tuck into a case.
- **Team kit.** If their team has a name, a matching shirt for the whole side is a fun group present from the captain or a partner.

## Darts jokes only a player gets: bed and breakfast, the 26 and the wrong side of the 20

Every regular knows the groan. You aim for treble 20 and land a single 20, a 5 and a 1, either side of the 20. That's 26, known on the oche as "bed and breakfast". Wikipedia's glossary of darts defines it as "a score of 26, made up of a single-5, single-20, single-1 in a game of x01", explaining that players aiming for the 20 "will often accidentally hit the 1 and the 5 sectors, which are located on either side of the 20" [9]. Wikipedia, which is not an official darts source, also says the name comes from the old price of a night's bed and breakfast: two shillings and sixpence, or "two and six" [9]. That is Wikipedia's explanation rather than one from a darts body, so treat it as a good pub story rather than settled history.

## Watching the World Championship at Christmas: gifts for the armchair fan

For many players, Christmas means the World Championship. The PDC's FAQ page gives the dates of the 2026/27 Paddy Power World Darts Championship at Alexandra Palace as 11 December 2026 to 3 January 2027 [3]. Its schedule lists no sessions on 24, 25 or 26 December, with play resuming on Sunday 27 December, and the final on Sunday 3 January [3]. Sky Sports describes this as "the usual Christmas break between December 24-27" [10].

Gift ideas for the armchair fan:

- **A cosy night in**: a blanket, a big mug and their favourite snacks for the evening sessions.
- **Tickets**, if you can get them and they'd enjoy the atmosphere at Alexandra Palace; check the PDC's own ticket information for details.
- **A darts book**, such as a player biography or a history of the game.
- **A home scoreboard** so they can play along with the checkouts between legs.

## Small stocking fillers

Small, inexpensive extras are often the gifts a player uses most. A few suggestions:

- A pack of flights or stems in the style they already use
- A [Bed and Breakfast Again sticker](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4595800640) for a darts case or laptop
- Chalk and a cloth for a chalk scoreboard, or pens for a wipe-clean one
- A checkout chart for the wall by the board
- A keyring or small case for spare flights
- A darts-themed Christmas card signed by the whole team
