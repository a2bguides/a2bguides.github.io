## Who this is for

This guide is for UK sole traders and very small trade businesses, such as plumbers, electricians, builders, decorators, gardeners and cleaners, who write quotes and invoices by hand or in a word processor. It gives you two free templates to copy, a checklist of what GOV.UK says an invoice must include, and a summary of what GOV.UK says about payment terms and late payment. Each rule is linked to the GOV.UK page it comes from.

### What a quote is for in practice

A quote is the written price you give a customer before you start work, so that you both know what has been agreed. In practice, a useful quote sets out:

- the price, and what that price is for
- what is included in the job
- what is not included (for example, materials the customer supplies, making good, or waste removal)
- how long the price is valid for
- your payment terms, such as a deposit, stage payments or payment on completion

Asking the customer to confirm in writing that they accept the quote before you start keeps a clear record of what was agreed. This guide does not cover the legal status of quotes or estimates; if that matters for a job, check with a professional.

## What an invoice must include

GOV.UK says an invoice is not the same as a receipt: a receipt acknowledges payment, while an invoice must include how much the customer needs to pay you and when they must pay you. GOV.UK also says you need to give an invoice by law if both you and the customer are registered for VAT (source: [GOV.UK, Invoicing and taking payment from customers](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers), checked 11 October 2026).

According to GOV.UK, every invoice must include the items below (source: [GOV.UK, Invoices: what they must include](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include), checked 11 October 2026).

**Every invoice:**

- a unique identification number
- your company name, address and contact information
- the company name and address of the customer you're invoicing
- a clear description of what you're charging for
- the date the goods or service were provided (supply date)
- the date of the invoice
- the amount(s) being charged
- VAT amount if applicable
- the total amount owed

**If you are a sole trader, also include:**

- your name and any business name being used
- if you use a business name, an address where any legal documents can be delivered to you

**If you are a limited company, also include:**

- the full company name as it appears on the certificate of incorporation
- if you choose to put directors' names on your invoices, the names of all directors

### How to use the templates for free

You do not need paid software to use these templates.

1. **Paste the template into a free tool you already have.** Google Docs, Microsoft Word or a notes app on your phone will all work. Using a fixed-width font such as Courier New keeps the price columns lined up.
2. **Save a master copy** with your own business details filled in, and leave the job details in [square brackets].
3. **Save a new copy for each job**, named so you can find it again, for example "Invoice 0007 – Smith – boiler service".
4. **Export as PDF before sending.** In Google Docs, Google's help page describes File, then Download, then PDF Document (.pdf) (source: [Google Docs Editors Help, Create, view, or download a file](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/49114), checked 11 October 2026). In Word for the web, Microsoft's help page describes File, then Export, then Download as PDF (source: [Microsoft Support, Export Word document as PDF](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/export-word-document-as-pdf), checked 11 October 2026).
5. **Number invoices in a simple sequence**, such as 0001, 0002, 0003, and never reuse a number. That way each invoice has the unique identification number GOV.UK lists as a required item. A separate sequence for quotes (Q0001, Q0002) avoids confusing the two.

## Payment terms and late payment

**Setting your own terms.** GOV.UK says you can set your own payment terms, such as discounts for early payment and payment upfront (source: [GOV.UK, Payment: obligations](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/payment-obligations), checked 11 October 2026). The "Payment terms" section of the quote template and the "Payment due by" line on the invoice are where you set these out.

**The 30-day default.** GOV.UK says that unless you agree a payment date, the customer must pay you within 30 days of getting your invoice or the goods or service. It also says you have the right to charge interest for late payment, but you can choose not to (source: [GOV.UK, Payment: obligations](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/payment-obligations), checked 11 October 2026).

**Late payment rules cover business customers only.** GOV.UK's late commercial payments guidance is about another business paying late for goods or services (business to business). It does not describe these rights for householders or other consumers. If your customer is a householder paying for work on their own home, GOV.UK does not describe the statutory interest and fixed sums below as applying to them (source: [GOV.UK, Late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery), checked 11 October 2026).

For business customers, the same GOV.UK guidance says:

- If no payment date is agreed, the payment is late 30 days after the later of: the customer getting the invoice, or you delivering the goods or providing the service.
- An agreed payment date is usually within 30 days for public authorities and 60 days for business transactions. Businesses can agree a longer period, but it must be fair to both businesses.
- If a business is late paying, you can claim interest and debt recovery costs.

(Source: [GOV.UK, Late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery), checked 11 October 2026.)

**Statutory interest (business to business only).** GOV.UK says statutory interest is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for business-to-business transactions. If a contract sets a different interest rate, that rate applies instead, and you cannot use a lower rate if you have a contract with a public authority. If you decide to add interest to the amount owed, GOV.UK says to send a new invoice (source: [GOV.UK, Charging interest on commercial debt](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/charging-interest-commercial-debt), checked 11 October 2026). This guide does not state the current base rate; check the Bank of England's published rate at the time you calculate interest.

**Fixed debt recovery sums (business to business only).** On top of interest, GOV.UK says you can charge a business that pays late a fixed sum, once per payment (source: [GOV.UK, Claim debt recovery costs on late payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/claim-debt-recovery-costs), checked 11 October 2026):

| Amount of debt | Fixed sum you can charge |
|---|---|
| Up to £999.99 | £40 |
| £1,000 to £9,999.99 | £70 |
| £10,000 or more | £100 |

## If you are VAT registered

GOV.UK says you must use VAT invoices if you and your customer are VAT registered, and that these include more information than non-VAT invoices (source: [GOV.UK, Invoices: what they must include](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include), checked 11 October 2026).

HMRC's VAT Notice 700/21 sets out the detail (source: [GOV.UK, Record keeping for VAT (VAT Notice 700/21)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/record-keeping-for-vat-notice-70021), checked 11 October 2026):

- **Time limit (section 3.1):** normally you must issue a VAT invoice within 30 days of the date you make the supply.
- **Full VAT invoice details (section 4.1):** these include a sequential number that uniquely identifies the document; the time of supply; the date of issue (where different to the time of supply); your name, address and VAT registration number; the customer's name and address; a description sufficient to identify the goods or services; the unit price; the rate of VAT; and the total amount of VAT chargeable, in sterling. Section 4.1 has further items, so check it for the full list.
- **Simplified invoices (section 4.5):** if the total value of the supply is £250 or less (including VAT), you may issue a simplified VAT invoice. Above £250 you must issue a full or modified VAT invoice.

The invoice template above has lines for a VAT registration number and a VAT amount, but it does not cover every item in section 4.1. If you are VAT registered, compare the template with section 4.1 of the notice and add any lines you need, such as a unit price or VAT rate for each item.
