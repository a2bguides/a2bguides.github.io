Someone you love has caught the clay bug. Their mugs are getting rounder, there is a faint grey tidemark on most of their clothes, and they talk about "trimming" and "bisque" as if everyone knows what those words mean. Buying for them can feel risky: pottery has lots of specialist kit, and the last thing you want is to wrap up a tool they already own, or a glaze they can't use. This guide covers gifts that are hard to get wrong, a few treats and experiences, and when it's worth asking before you spend.

## Before you buy: where does your potter work?

It helps to know where they make their pots, because that shapes what will be useful. Set-ups vary a great deal, so treat the points below as questions to ask rather than rules.

**If they go to a class or shared studio,** find out what the studio provides and what they bring along themselves. If they carry their own kit, gifts that travel well may suit them: a tool roll or small bag, an apron, sponges, or a voucher for more studio time.

**If they have a wheel at home,** ask, or have a quiet look, at what they already own, so you add variety rather than duplicates: a differently shaped rib, a different trimming tool, or something to make their space nicer to work in.

If you're not sure which they are, a casual "where do you fire your pots?" is an easy way to find out.

## Small tools and kit to consider

Small tools and studio kit make good stocking fillers. How long any of them lasts depends on the tool and how it is used, so check what they already have before you choose.

- **Sponges.** A pack of pottery sponges, perhaps in a few different types, is a simple stocking filler.
- **Ribs.** Ribs are flat, hand-held tools used to smooth and shape clay. They come in metal, wood, rubber and plastic, in different shapes and degrees of stiffness. A rib in a shape or firmness they don't have yet adds variety, even if they already own a few.
- **Trimming tools.** Trimming (or turning) is how potters carve a foot ring into the base of a pot once it has firmed up. A tool with a different head shape from the ones they have can add variety. Have a quiet look at what's already in their kit before choosing.
- **Aprons.** Clay gets everywhere, and an apron with pockets is a practical idea for class and home potters alike. For something with a bit of humour, our [The Kiln Gods Took Three apron](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4598189498) carries the same kiln joke as the tee and sticker further down.
- **Storage.** A tool roll, a canvas bag for carrying kit to class, or a few lidded tubs for home trimmings all help tame the clutter.

## Classes, studio time and experiences

Experiences can make a good gift for a keen beginner, especially one who wants more time at the wheel.

**Courses and taster sessions.** A voucher for a term of classes, a weekend workshop or a one-day taster in something new (hand-building, throwing larger forms, surface decoration) is hard to get wrong. The Craft Potters Association (CPA) keeps a list of ceramic courses split into "Colleges & Institutions" and "Private Courses", and says these "offer courses ranging from those without accreditation through to BA, MA and Phd level study" ([CPA Ceramic Courses](https://www.craftpotters.com/ceramic-courses/), checked 18 October 2026). The same page notes that "details of adult education ceramic and pottery courses are listed on local government websites", which is a good starting point for UK readers looking for local classes ([CPA Ceramic Courses](https://www.craftpotters.com/ceramic-courses/), checked 18 October 2026). In the US, community art centres and local studios are worth a look.

**CPA membership.** The CPA says that membership "is available to individual makers and to anyone interested in studio ceramics", and its Associate Membership is aimed at anyone from "a maker, a collector, student, gallery or an enthusiast for ceramics" ([CPA Membership](https://www.craftpotters.com/membership/), checked 18 October 2026). Benefits listed on its home page include "Exhibitions and events, including Ceramic Art London and CPA fairs" and "Access to the CPA's wider programme, including publishing through Ceramic Review" ([Craft Potters Association](https://www.craftpotters.com/), checked 18 October 2026). Check current membership prices with the CPA before you buy.

**A day at a ceramics fair.** A fair is a lovely outing for anyone who makes pots, and a chance to see the work of experienced makers up close.

- **Oxford Ceramics Fair** runs on Saturday 31 October and Sunday 1 November 2026 at St Edward's School, Oxford OX2 7NN, according to the Craft Potters Association's home page, which also lists "63 exceptional ceramic artists and potters" and "six live talks and demonstrations from exhibiting makers", all included in the ticket ([Craft Potters Association home page](https://www.craftpotters.com/), checked 20 October 2026). According to the Oxford Ceramics Fair's official Tickets page, advance tickets are £9.00 full price or £7.50 concessions, each plus a 75p booking fee; on the door they are £11.00 and £9.50. Concessions cover CPA members, unemployed people, students and registered disabled people, with proof of status required. Under-16s go free but still need a ticket, and the door takes cards only ([Oxford Ceramics Fair, official Tickets page](https://www.oxfordceramicsfair.com/tickets), checked 20 October 2026). It falls well before Christmas, so it suits a pre-Christmas day out, or a trip to buy a handmade piece for your potter.
- **York Ceramics Fair** is listed for 27 February 2027 (10am to 5pm) and 28 February 2027 (10am to 4pm) at York Racecourse, Knavesmire Road, York YO23 1EX ([York Ceramics Fair](https://www.yorkceramicsfair.com/), checked 18 October 2026). Parts of the site still showed the previous year's dates, so confirm before booking.
- **Ceramic Art London**, "Produced by the Craft Potters Association of Great Britain", is listed for Thursday 6 to Sunday 9 May 2027 at Olympia (West Hall) ([Ceramic Art London](https://www.ceramicartlondon.com/), checked 18 October 2026). The site still showed the previous year's dates in places, so again, confirm before booking. A card promising a trip there makes a nice thing to open on Christmas morning.

## Books, magazines and inspiration

**A Ceramic Review subscription.** Ceramic Review is described by the CPA as "The international magazine for ceramics, ceramic art, and studio pottery" ([Craft Potters Association](https://www.craftpotters.com/), checked 18 October 2026). The print edition is bi-monthly. A one-year UK print subscription is £49.95 (or £44.95 for CPA members), two years is £89.90, and a one-year overseas subscription, which would cover US readers, is £66.95. A one-year digital-only subscription is £24.99. Print subscribers also get free online access to the whole archive, "going all the way back to our first issue in 1970", and subscriptions bought after a publication date start with the next issue ([Ceramic Review subscribe](https://www.ceramicreview.com/subscribe/), checked 18 October 2026). The subscribe page does not mention gift subscriptions, so if you want it to arrive as a surprise, contact Ceramic Review before ordering to ask how they handle gift orders.

**Books.** A well-illustrated pottery book suits the coffee table as well as the studio shelf. A beginner might enjoy a clear step-by-step guide to throwing or hand-building, while a more experienced potter might prefer a book about a particular maker, style or glaze tradition.

**Inspiration.** A handmade mug, bowl or vase from a potter they admire, bought at a fair or open studio, is a gift that also teaches: they can study how someone else trimmed a foot or pulled a handle.

## Funny gifts for when the kiln eats their work

The reasons a piece can fail in the kiln get technical, and this guide doesn't try to explain them; your potter is the best person to ask. ## Glazes, wheels and kilns: worth a conversation first

These are bigger, more specialised purchases, and what suits one potter may not suit another. A short conversation before buying is the simplest way to avoid a gift that doesn't fit.

- **Glazes.** Ask which glazes they would like, and whether their studio has rules about what can go in its kilns. A voucher from the supplier they already use is an alternative.
- **Wheels.** Ask which model they have in mind, and where it would go. A contribution towards one, or a card saying "let's choose your wheel together", lets them choose.
- **Kilns.** If they are thinking about a kiln, treat it as a joint decision rather than a surprise.

The same goes for large tool sets. If they've mentioned a particular tool, that may be a better choice than a big kit that could overlap with what they already own.

## Gift ideas by budget

**Under £10 / $15**
- Spare pottery sponges
- A single rib or small trimming tool
- A funny sticker or a pottery-themed card
- An advance ticket to Oxford Ceramics Fair (£9.00 plus a 75p booking fee) ([Oxford Ceramics Fair, official Tickets page](https://www.oxfordceramicsfair.com/tickets), checked 20 October 2026)

**£10 to £30 / $15 to $40**
- A one-year Ceramic Review digital subscription (£24.99) ([Ceramic Review subscribe](https://www.ceramicreview.com/subscribe/), checked 18 October 2026)
- A funny tee or a good apron
- A specialist trimming tool or a set of ribs in different shapes
- A handmade mug from a potter they admire

**£30 to £75 / $40 to $100**
- A one-year Ceramic Review print subscription (£49.95 UK, £66.95 overseas) ([Ceramic Review subscribe](https://www.ceramicreview.com/subscribe/), checked 18 October 2026)
- A canvas tool roll filled with a few new tools
- A well-illustrated pottery book plus a fair ticket
- A one-day workshop or taster session

**Bigger treats**
- A term of classes or a block of studio time
- A trip to Ceramic Art London in May 2027
- A contribution towards a wheel or kiln, agreed with them first
